For grown-ups

Sharing the Bible with a 6- to 10-year-old

You don’t need to be an expert. Children this age mostly need a grown-up who reads with them, listens to their questions and shows what trusting God looks like in ordinary life.

How kids this age think

🧱 Concrete thinkers

Children of 6 to 10 understand best through things they can picture, touch and do. “God is with you” lands better tied to a real moment, like the dark bedroom or the first day of school, than as an idea on its own.

📖 Story first

They remember stories with people, problems and rescues far better than lists of facts. Tell the Bible as one connected story, and keep coming back to “what does this show us about God?”

⚖️ A strong sense of fair

“That’s not fair!” is a big deal at this age. Lean into it: the Bible cares deeply about fairness, and grace is the surprising good news that God gives us better than we deserve.

🤝 Belonging matters

Kids are working out who they are and where they fit. Help them hear that they are made on purpose, loved by God and part of his family, before they hear what they should do.

❓ Questions are good

Lots of “why?” and “how?” is a sign of a growing faith, not a lack of it. “That’s a great question, let’s find out together” is always a good answer, and so is “I don’t know.”

🌀 Short and moving

Attention runs about 10 to 15 minutes. Keep it short, use hand motions and drawing, and repeat favorites. Hearing the same story many times is how children learn it.

A simple 10-minute family time

Pick a part of the Big Story and walk through these four steps. Bedtime, breakfast or the car all work.

1 · ReadRead the short story out loud, then the Bible verse. Let your child read the verse if they can.
2 · WonderAsk one “I wonder…” question. Listen more than you talk. There are no wrong answers here.
3 · PraySay the short prayer together, or let your child pray in their own words.
4 · DoTry the hand motion and the activity. Doing helps remembering.

Hard stories and big feelings

Scary parts

The Bible is honest about danger, war and death. Preview a passage first, tell it at your child’s level, and always land on what God does: he protects, rescues and keeps his promises. A good children’s Bible helps with this.

When they feel bad about doing wrong

Children this age can feel guilt strongly. Pair honesty about sin with the bigger truth: God forgives, and nothing they do makes him stop loving them (Romans 8:39).

When life is hard

A move, a loss or a fight at school is a chance to show that God’s story includes sad parts too, and that God is with us in them. The Psalms give words for every feeling.
