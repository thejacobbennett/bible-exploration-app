For grown-ups

Exploring the Bible with a 6- to 10-year-old

At this age children can read for themselves, love to collect, sort and solve, and ask sharp questions. This page is built around that: clear missions, real Bible passages to look up, quick quizzes and badges.

How kids this age think

Logical, but concrete

Between about 7 and 11, children start reasoning logically about real things: order, cause and effect, categories. Abstract ideas land best when tied to a story, a place, a date or something they can do.

Collectors and sorters

They love lists, timelines, facts, badges and “how many?” Use that: let them put the story in order, sort the books of the Bible and track what they’ve finished.

Competence matters

Psychologists describe this as the age of “I can do it myself.” Let them find the passage in their own Bible, read it aloud and explain it back to you. Praise effort and curiosity, not only right answers.

Fairness and right and wrong

Rules and fairness feel huge. The Bible’s stories of justice, forgiveness and grace give rich material: grace is getting better than we deserve.

Friends and belonging

Peers matter more each year. Point to the church as a family they belong to, and to Bible friendships like David and Jonathan or Ruth and Naomi.

Real questions

“Is it true?” and “How do we know?” are healthy. Take them seriously, answer honestly, and it’s fine to say “I don’t know, let’s find out together.”

How to use the missions

1 · Read

Your child reads the story (or you read together), then looks up the passage in a real Bible. A readable children’s or full Bible both work.

2 · Talk

Use the “Think about it” questions. Listen more than you talk. Ask “What does this show us about God?”

3 · Do

Take the quiz, try the Explorer Challenge, pray together and earn the badge. One mission a day or one a week both work well.
